### Step 2: Crafting Effective Prompts for AI Video Generation

The quality of your InVideo AI output depends heavily on the prompt. Here is what we learned from generating 25 videos:

**Good prompt structure:**
```
[Video type] about [topic] for [audience].
Tone: [professional/casual/energetic/educational].
Length: [30 seconds / 1 minute / 3 minutes].
Include: [specific elements you want].
Style: [modern/minimalist/corporate/playful].
```

**Example of an effective prompt:**
```
A 1-minute YouTube explainer about how solar panels work for homeowners.
Tone: educational but approachable.
Include: a comparison of cost vs. savings over 5 years, common installation questions, and environmental impact.
Style: modern with clean infographics.
Voice: male, US English.
```

**Example of a weak prompt (what to avoid):**
```
Make a video about solar panels.
```

The detailed prompt produced a structured video with clear sections (intro, how it works, cost analysis, environmental benefits, conclusion), relevant stock footage of solar installations, and data overlay graphics. The vague prompt generated a generic slideshow with loosely related imagery.

**Failure case:** When we asked InVideo AI to create a "comparison video between electric cars and gas cars with detailed specs," the AI generated a script that mentioned specific numbers (e.g., "Tesla Model 3 has 358 miles of range") — but these numbers were not verified. For factual content, always review and correct AI-generated statistics before publishing.

### Step 3: Editing and Refining the AI-Generated Video

Once InVideo generates your video (typically 2-5 minutes for a 1-minute video), you enter the editing interface:

**Scene-level editing:**
1. Each scene appears as a thumbnail in the timeline at the bottom. Click any scene to edit it
2. **Replace media**: Click the stock footage or image in a scene, then search the built-in library (powered by Storyblocks and Shutterstock on paid plans) or upload your own files
3. **Adjust timing**: Drag scene edges in the timeline to lengthen or shorten. The voiceover and subtitles automatically adjust
4. **Edit script**: Click the text panel on the left to modify the voiceover script. Changes sync to both voiceover and subtitles

**Voiceover customization:**
- Change voice: Select from 40+ AI voices across male, female, and multiple accents (US, UK, AU, Indian English)
- Adjust speed: Faster for energetic ads, slower for educational content
- Upload your own voiceover: Replace AI voiceover entirely with a recorded audio file

**Branding elements:**
- Add a logo watermark in the Brand Kit settings (Plus and Max plans)
- Set brand colors for text overlays and lower thirds
- Upload custom intro/outro clips

**Pro tip:** Generate the AI video first as a "rough cut," then spend your editing time replacing 2-3 weakest scenes with custom media. This gives you 80% of the quality of a fully manual edit at 20% of the time investment.

### Step 4: Exporting and Publishing Your Video

**Export settings:**
1. Click "Export" in the top right
2. Select resolution: 720p, 1080p (recommended), or 4K (Max plan)
3. Choose aspect ratio: 16:9 (YouTube), 9:16 (TikTok/Reels/Shorts), or 1:1 (Instagram)
4. Wait for rendering — a 1-minute video takes roughly 2-3 minutes at 1080p

**Direct publishing (available on paid plans):**
- YouTube: Connect your channel and publish directly with title, description, and tags
- Facebook/Instagram: Connect social accounts for direct posting
- Download: Save as MP4 to your device for manual upload anywhere

**Workflow for multi-platform content:**
1. Generate the video in 16:9 for YouTube
2. Use the "Resize" feature to create a 9:16 version for Shorts/TikTok — InVideo AI reframes scenes automatically, but review each scene since important visual elements may be cropped
3. Export both versions separately

During testing, the auto-reframing for vertical format worked well for scenes with centered subjects but struggled with wide landscape shots. Budget 5-10 minutes to manually adjust vertical crops.

## FAQ

### How is InVideo AI different from traditional video editors?

Traditional editors (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut) give you frame-level control — you place every clip, adjust every transition, and manually sync audio. InVideo AI writes the script, selects the footage, adds voiceover, and syncs subtitles automatically. It trades granular control for speed. Think of it as a first-draft generator rather than a replacement for professional editing software.

### Can I use my own footage in InVideo AI?

On paid plans, you can upload your own video clips, images, and audio files to replace AI-selected stock media. You can also upload a custom voiceover to replace the AI-generated voice. The free tier has limited upload capability.

### Does InVideo AI support multiple languages?

InVideo AI supports video generation in 50+ languages. The AI can generate scripts, voiceovers, and subtitles in languages including Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Japanese, and Korean. Voiceover quality is strongest in English, but other widely spoken languages produce usable results with occasional accent inconsistencies.

### How long does it take to generate a video?

A 1-minute video typically takes 2-5 minutes to generate. Longer videos (3-5 minutes) may take 5-10 minutes. Export rendering adds another 2-5 minutes depending on resolution and video length.

### Is there a limit on how many videos I can create?

The free tier limits AI generation to 10 minutes per week. The Plus plan includes 50 minutes per month. The Max plan includes 200 minutes per month. Video storage is unlimited on all paid plans.

### Can I cancel my subscription anytime?

InVideo AI is a subscription service billed monthly or annually. You can cancel anytime, and access continues until the end of the current billing period. There is no long-term contract — but there is also no refund for partial months.

### Does InVideo AI work on mobile?

There is no dedicated mobile app, but the web platform is responsive and works on mobile browsers. For serious editing, a desktop or laptop is recommended due to the complexity of the timeline interface on small screens.
