**Interface layout:**

## FAQ

### How is Consensus different from Google Scholar?

Google Scholar searches paper metadata (title, abstract, author) and returns a list of papers — it is a discovery tool. Consensus uses AI to read and extract findings from the papers it indexes, then answers questions directly with synthesized results. Google Scholar covers more papers and is better for exhaustive literature searches. Consensus is faster for getting an evidence-based answer to a specific question and understanding the directional weight of research.

### Does Consensus only search peer-reviewed papers?

Consensus primarily indexes peer-reviewed journals, preprint servers (arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv), and some conference proceedings. It labels preprints clearly as "Preprint" so you can distinguish them from peer-reviewed work. For fields where preprints are standard (computer science, physics), this is useful. For medical decisions, peer-reviewed papers should carry more weight.

### How does the AI extract key findings from papers?

Consensus uses proprietary NLP models trained on academic papers to read the abstract and full text (when available) and extract the paper's primary finding in one sentence. The extraction focuses on the conclusion or key result rather than background or methodology. The system performs well on papers with clear, explicit conclusions — it is less reliable for papers with nuanced or heavily qualified conclusions.

### Can I use Consensus for systematic reviews?

Consensus can accelerate the screening phase of a systematic review by quickly surfacing relevant papers and their key findings. However, it should not replace a formal systematic review methodology: Consensus does not guarantee exhaustive coverage of all databases, and its search algorithm is not transparent in the way PRISMA guidelines require. Use it as a discovery and triage tool, not as the sole search method for a published systematic review.

### Is my search data private?

Consensus states that it does not sell user data and that search history is private by default. Premium users can opt into sharing anonymized search data to improve the platform. For research on sensitive topics, review the privacy policy or use the platform without an account (limited functionality).

### How current is the research in Consensus?

Consensus indexes papers continuously, with new publications typically appearing within days to weeks of publication. Preprints from arXiv and bioRxiv appear within 24-48 hours. However, the depth of coverage varies by field — biomedical sciences are covered most comprehensively, while humanities and social sciences have more limited coverage.

### What happens if I ask a question with no published research?

Consensus will return a message indicating insufficient research to answer the question. This is a useful signal in itself — it tells you that the topic has not been studied rigorously or that you may need to rephrase your question. Unlike general search engines that always return something, Consensus says "we do not have enough evidence" when the research is thin.
