### Step 4: Setting Upscale Parameters

After selecting the model, configure the upscale settings:

- **Upscale factor:** 2x, 4x, 8x, or 16x. The maximum factor depends on your plan and the original image resolution. For most practical use cases, 2x–4x provides a meaningful quality improvement without extreme file sizes.
- **Output format:** JPEG, PNG, or WebP. PNG is recommended for images that will be further edited; JPEG at quality 90–95 is suitable for final delivery.
- **Face enhancement toggle (Photo model):** When enabled, the model applies additional processing to facial features for portrait images.

**Practical tip:** For e-commerce product photos destined for a website, a 2x upscale from 800px to 1600px width with JPEG output is typically sufficient. For print, target the pixel dimensions required by your printer (300 DPI × physical inches).

## Failure Case: The Over-Aggressive 16x Upscale

**The Source Image:**

A 600×400 pixel JPEG of a group photo from a company event, taken with a budget smartphone in indoor lighting. The original already showed visible noise in shadow areas and soft focus on faces in the back row.

**What Went Wrong:**

We applied a 16x upscale (to 9600×6400 pixels) using the Photo model with face enhancement enabled, hoping to extract enough detail for a large-format print. The result was technically larger — but visually problematic:

- Background faces (already soft in the original) became waxy, featureless shapes — the AI "hallucinated" smooth skin texture where no detail existed to recover
- Noise in shadow areas was misinterpreted as texture and amplified, creating a granular, speckled pattern on dark clothing
- Face enhancement overcorrected front-row faces, producing an unnatural, over-processed look resembling excessive beauty filter application
- The final file was 78MB — impractical for most delivery channels

**How We Fixed It:**

We reprocessed the image with three adjustments:

1. Reduced the upscale factor to 4x (2400×1600) — a more realistic target given the source quality
2. Disabled face enhancement — the Photo model's base processing was sufficient for faces at this modest upscale factor
3. Used JPEG output at 90% quality instead of PNG — reducing file size from 78MB to 1.8MB

The 4x output was not suitable for a 24×36-inch poster, but worked well for an 8×10-inch team photo print and web use. Key lesson: upscale factor should be proportional to source image quality. A 600px smartphone photo in poor lighting does not contain 9600px worth of real detail — applying an extreme upscale factor amplifies noise and artifacts rather than revealing hidden information.

## FAQ

### 1. Does Let's Enhance work on all image types?

Let's Enhance supports JPEG, PNG, WebP, and TIFF formats. However, the quality of the output depends significantly on the source image quality and the AI model selected. Product photos, portraits, and illustrations produce the strongest results. Heavily compressed, low-resolution, or extremely noisy source images will show limited improvement regardless of the upscale factor — the AI can only work with the information present in the original.

### 2. What is the difference between "Smart Enhance" and the other models?

Smart Enhance is the general-purpose default — it analyzes the image and selects enhancement parameters automatically. The specialized models (Photo, Digital Art, Text & Scan) are trained on specific image categories and apply category-appropriate processing. In our testing, selecting the specialized model for the image type (Photo for product shots, Digital Art for illustrations) produced noticeably better detail preservation than Smart Enhance alone.

### 3. Can I upscale an image for large-format printing?

Yes, but with an important caveat: the source image must have sufficient base quality. A well-lit 12MP smartphone photo can reasonably be upscaled to 24×36 inches at 200–300 DPI. A noisy 3MP web image upscaled to the same print size will show visible artifacts. Calculate your target pixel dimensions (print inches × desired DPI) and compare with your source resolution to set realistic expectations.

### 4. Is Let's Enhance suitable for restoring old scanned photos?

For scans with moderate noise, fading, and small scratches, the Photo model produces acceptable results — it reduces grain and improves contrast. For severely damaged photos (tears, large stains, missing sections), Let's Enhance is not the appropriate tool — dedicated photo restoration services or manual Photoshop retouching are more suitable. The Text & Scan model works well for improving the readability of scanned documents.

### 5. Does batch processing apply the same settings to all images?

By default, yes — all images in a batch receive the same model and upscale settings. However, you can configure per-image settings by clicking individual thumbnails before processing. For mixed catalogs (product photos alongside lifestyle images), per-image configuration is recommended to match the appropriate AI model to each image type.

### 6. How does Let's Enhance handle faces in group photos?

The optional face enhancement toggle in the Photo model applies targeted processing to detected faces. For close-up portraits, this generally improves eye sharpness and skin texture. For group photos where faces occupy a small portion of the frame, the results are more variable — distant faces may receive less effective enhancement, and very small faces (<50px wide) may show unnatural smoothing. We recommend testing on a single group photo before batch-processing an entire event album.

### 7. Can I cancel my subscription after processing a large batch?

Let's Enhance offers monthly and annual subscriptions. You can cancel a monthly subscription at any time — your credits remain available until the end of the current billing period. If you have a one-time batch processing need (e.g., a catalog upgrade), a single month of the paid plan is sufficient.
