## Core Tutorial: Creating Professional Music with Suno v4

### Step 1: Understanding Suno's Generation Modes

Suno offers two generation modes, which determine how the song is built:

- **Custom Mode:** You provide both lyrics and a style prompt. Suno generates the full song — vocals, instrumentation, arrangement — based on your inputs. This is the primary mode for complete songs.
- **Instrumental Mode:** No lyrics. You specify only a style prompt, and Suno generates a purely instrumental track. Suitable for background music, soundtracks, and ambient pieces.

In custom mode, Suno v4 supports explicit song structure notation within lyrics using tags like `[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`, `[Outro]`, and stylistic directions like `[Guitar solo]` or `[Fade out]`. These tags are parsed and influence the final arrangement — they are not merely decorative.

### Step 2: Writing Effective Style Prompts

The style prompt controls genre, mood, tempo, instrumentation, and vocal characteristics. Suno v4 supports multi-genre blending (up to 3 genres simultaneously):

**Strong style prompt structure:**
1. **Primary genre + sub-genre:** "Indie folk with Americana influences"
2. **Mood and energy:** "Wistful, mid-tempo, driving rhythm"
3. **Instrumentation:** "Acoustic guitar, brushed drums, harmonica, upright bass"
4. **Vocal style:** "Male vocals, warm baritone, intimate delivery"
5. **Reference era/artist feel (optional):** "Early Bon Iver, Iron & Wine"

Example:

> Indie folk, Americana, wistful, mid-tempo. Acoustic guitar fingerpicking, brushed drums, harmonica fills, upright bass. Male vocals, warm baritone, intimate close-mic delivery. Autumn evening campfire atmosphere. Early Mumford & Sons, The Head and the Heart.

This prompt produced a track in our test with authentic fingerpicked acoustic guitar, tasteful harmonica accents, and vocals that sounded convincingly human — including natural breath sounds and slight pitch variations that avoided the robotic quality of earlier AI music tools.

**What works:** Specific instrumentation ("Fender Rhodes electric piano" over "keyboard"), descriptive mood terms ("melancholic," "triumphant," "hazy"), genre blends ("lo-fi hip-hop meets bossa nova").

**What to avoid:** Abstract metaphors ("sounds like rainbows"), contradictory instructions ("slow and fast"), and overly long prompts (keep under 250 characters for best results).

### Step 3: Structuring Lyrics for Song Coherence

Suno parses structured lyrics to build verse-chorus-verse arrangements. A well-structured lyric input:

```
[Verse 1]
Streetlights flicker on the empty avenue
I'm tracing footsteps that don't lead to you
The city's quiet in a way it never was
Before I learned to count the things I've lost

[Pre-Chorus]
And the radio plays something slow and sad
The kind of song you said you always hated

[Chorus]
But I'm still standing where you left me
Hands in pockets, heart unsteady
Every headlight looks like your car
Every stranger sounds like who you are

[Verse 2]
The coffee shop changed owners back in May
I still order like you're coming anyway
...
```

Suno v4 respects this structure well. In our test, the generated track clearly distinguished verses (stripped-back instrumentation, intimate vocals) from choruses (full band enters, vocal intensity rises, harmonies added). The pre-chorus built tension with a rising chord progression and drum fill before the chorus drop — structurally sophisticated arrangement that felt musically intentional rather than randomly generated.

### Step 4: Using Persona Mode for Sonic Continuity

Persona mode (new in v4) captures the sonic fingerprint of a generated track — vocal timbre, instrumental palette, production style — and applies it to subsequent generations:

1. Generate a track you like
2. Click "Create Persona" on the track
3. Name the persona (e.g., "Folk Singer-Songwriter Voice")
4. Use the persona in future prompts by referencing it

In our test, we created a persona from an indie folk track and used it to generate 3 additional songs in different styles (a ballad, an upbeat Americana track, and a duet). The persona maintained vocal timbre and the core instrumental palette (acoustic guitar, upright bass, brushed drums) while adapting to each song's mood — a capability that enables album-like sonic cohesion across multiple tracks.

**Limitation:** Personas do not transfer perfectly between drastically different genres. Moving from folk to synthwave with the same persona produces mixed results — the vocal character carries over, but the instrumental palette shift can feel jarring.

### Step 5: Extending, Remixing, and Exporting Tracks

Suno provides post-generation tools for refining tracks:

- **Extend:** Add a new section (intro, outro, bridge, solo) to an existing track. Suno analyzes the existing audio and generates a seamless continuation.
- **Remix:** Reinterpret a track in a different style while preserving the core melody or structure. Strength slider from Subtle (keep most elements) to Dramatic (radical reinterpretation).
- **Cover Song:** Generate a cover version with different instrumentation or vocal style.
- **Export:** Download as MP3 (Pro plan) or WAV (Premier plan). Instrumental stems are available on the Premier plan.

In our podcast intro test, we extended an 8-second instrumental clip to 30 seconds by adding an intro swell and outro fade using the Extend feature. The transition points were seamless — no audible clicks, consistent reverb tail, smooth volume curve. The resulting 30-second clip worked as a podcast intro without any DAW editing.

## Failure Case: The Jazz Fusion Trap

**The Prompt:**

> Jazz fusion, 70s Herbie Hancock style. Fender Rhodes, fretless bass, soprano saxophone, complex time signatures. Up-tempo, improvisational, energetic live club atmosphere.

**What Went Wrong:**

The generated track attempted to blend all requested elements simultaneously and produced something that sounded less like jazz fusion and more like programmatic chaos. The Fender Rhodes comping was rhythmically rigid (no swing feel, no dynamic variation), the soprano sax sounded synthesized rather than breathy, the fretless bass slides were pitch-imprecise in a way that felt wrong rather than expressive, and the complex time signature changes (which Suno attempted — credits for ambition) were jarring rather than fluid. The track was musically incoherent — the worst-performing generation in our entire test suite.

**How We Fixed It:**

We analyzed what went wrong. Jazz fusion requires improvisational nuance, dynamic interplay between instruments, and rhythmic fluidity — all areas where AI music generation remains underdeveloped relative to structured genres like pop or folk. The fix was genre selection, not prompt refinement:

1. We switched to a simpler jazz-adjacent genre: "Acoustic jazz ballad, smoky lounge atmosphere. Piano trio, brushed drums, walking bass. Late-night, intimate, melancholy."
2. We removed all improvisation-heavy elements: no saxophone, no complex time signatures, no electric instruments.
3. We focused the prompt on mood and atmosphere rather than technical musical attributes.

The revised generation produced a competent piano trio ballad with tasteful brush work and a legible walking bass line — musically coherent and pleasant to listen to, even if not virtuosic. The score rose from 3.5 to 4.5 for musical coherence.

The broader lesson: Suno performs reliably for structured genres with clear conventions (pop, rock, folk, electronic, country). For genres requiring improvisational interplay and rhythmic subtlety (jazz, classical chamber music, progressive metal), results are inconsistent. Choose genres strategically based on Suno's documented strengths.

## FAQ

### 1. Do I own the music I generate with Suno?

Suno's terms grant paid subscribers (Pro and Premier) commercial ownership of generated tracks, meaning you can use them in monetized content including YouTube videos, podcasts, and commercial projects. Free-tier generations are for non-commercial use only. The broader question of AI-generated music copyright is still being debated legally, and policies on major platforms (Spotify, YouTube Content ID) continue to evolve. For critical commercial projects, consulting an IP attorney is recommended.

### 2. Can I download individual instrument stems?

Yes, on the Premier plan ($30/month). Suno provides separated stems for vocals and instrumentation. The separation quality is moderate — usable for basic remixing and content creation, but not at the level of professionally recorded multi-track sessions. For projects requiring precise stem isolation, post-processing with dedicated stem separation tools may be necessary.

### 3. How many songs can I generate per month?

The Pro plan ($10/month) provides 500 credits, which translates to approximately 250 songs (2 credits per song generation). The Premier plan ($30/month) provides 2,000 credits (~1,000 songs). The free tier provides 50 credits per day (~25 songs). Credits are consumed per generation attempt, so refining a song through multiple generations counts against your quota.

### 4. Can Suno generate music in languages other than English?

Yes, Suno supports lyrics in many languages. However, vocal quality varies. Our testing found strong results for English, Spanish, French, and Japanese. Less common languages and languages with complex tonal systems may produce accented or mispronounced vocals. When generating non-English vocals, simpler syllable structures produce better results.

### 5. Does Suno integrate with DAWs or music production software?

Suno does not offer direct DAW integration. The workflow is: generate in Suno → download as MP3 or WAV → import into your DAW (Logic Pro, Ableton, FL Studio, etc.) for editing, mixing, and mastering. The Premier plan's stem export feature partially bridges this gap by providing separated vocal and instrumental tracks for DAW mixing, but there is no real-time round-trip between Suno and any DAW.
