## Core Tutorial: Getting Started with Speechify in 2026

### Step 1: Setting Up Your Account and Installing the Browser Extension

Visit [speechify.com](https://speechify.com) and create an account. The free tier provides access to a limited set of standard voices — enough to evaluate the core experience. Premium ($139/year) unlocks the full HD voice library, cross-platform sync, and advanced features like OCR and scanning.

Install the Chrome extension — it is Speechify's most seamless entry point:

1. Search "Speechify" in the Chrome Web Store or visit the link from the Speechify dashboard
2. Click "Add to Chrome" and pin the extension to your toolbar
3. Click the Speechify icon and sign in with your account
4. The extension adds a floating play button to supported web pages

**Screenshot description:** *Chrome browser with the Speechify extension pinned in the toolbar. A web article page shows a floating Speechify play button in the bottom-right corner. Clicking it opens a mini-player showing the current paragraph highlighted in yellow as it reads aloud.*

### Step 2: Choosing the Right Voice for Your Content Type

Speechify's voice library is the core of the experience. In the settings panel:

1. Navigate to Voice Settings (the person icon in the player)
2. Browse available voices — Premium users see 30+ HD voices across multiple providers
3. Preview each voice with a sample sentence before selecting
4. Adjust speed (0.5x–4.5x in 0.1x increments using the slider)

Key voices we found most natural:

- **Salli (US English, female)** — Neutral, clear, works well for business and technical content
- **Matthew (US English, male)** — Slightly deeper timbre, good for narrative and long-form books
- **Joanna (US English, female)** — Natural conversational cadence for casual content

Voice quality differences are noticeable: the HD voices on Premium have more natural intonation and fewer mechanical artifacts compared with the free-tier standard voices.

**Screenshot description:** *Speechify voice settings panel showing a grid of voice cards with play buttons for preview. Salli and Matthew are visible with voice provider labels underneath. The speed slider is set to 1.5x, and a waveform visualization animates as the preview plays.*

### Step 3: Reading Web Articles and Saving for Later

Speechify's web reading workflow works as follows:

1. Navigate to any article page and click the floating play button or the extension icon
2. Speechify extracts the main content (bypassing ads, nav bars, sidebars) and begins reading from the top
3. The current sentence is highlighted in yellow as it reads — helpful for visual tracking
4. Use keyboard shortcuts: Space to play/pause, left/right arrows to skip backward/forward by sentence
5. Click "Save" to add the article to your Speechify library for later listening across devices

The auto-scroll feature keeps the page aligned with the current reading position. On long articles (3,000+ words), we found 1.5x speed provided a good balance between speed and comprehension — approximately 300 words per minute with high retention.

**Screenshot description:** *Web article page with Speechify reading mode active. The current sentence is highlighted in yellow, the page has auto-scrolled to keep the active sentence centered. The mini-player at the bottom shows the article title, current position (12:45 / 28:30), and playback controls.*

### Step 4: Uploading PDFs, EPUBs, and Scanned Documents

Speechify handles file uploads through the web dashboard and mobile app:

1. Go to the Speechify web app > Library > Upload
2. Drag and drop PDF, EPUB, DOCX, or TXT files (files up to 200MB supported on Premium)
3. Speechify processes the file — extracting text and converting it into a listenable format
4. For image-based or scanned PDFs, Speechify's OCR engine extracts text before narration (Premium feature)

In our PDF testing, text-based PDFs processed accurately with good formatting preservation. Scanned PDFs with clear text at 300+ DPI were readable approximately 85% of the time. Low-resolution scans or documents with heavy background patterns produced garbled or incomplete OCR results.

The PDF file shows a "Listening" status with a progress bar at 45%. A thumbnail preview of the PDF cover is visible next to each file.*

### Step 5: Mobile Listening and Cross-Platform Sync

The Speechify iOS and Android apps provide the listening-on-the-go experience:

1. Download the app and sign in with the same account
2. Your library syncs — saved articles, uploaded files, and listening positions carry over
3. Use the mobile widget for quick playback control from the lock screen
4. For physical documents, use the Scan feature — point your camera at a page and Speechify reads it aloud via OCR

The cross-platform sync was reliable in our testing: pausing an article on the web at 60% and resuming on mobile picked up exactly where we left off, with a delay of under 5 seconds for position sync. This makes Speechify practical for "start on desktop, finish on mobile" reading habits.

## Failure Case: When OCR Failed on a Scanned Contract Document

**The Scenario:**

We uploaded a scanned PDF of a 12-page service contract — a document scanned at 200 DPI from a printed original with light gray background and small (10pt) text. The contract contained legal formatting including numbered clauses, indented sub-clauses, and signature blocks.

**What Went Wrong:**

Speechify's OCR processed approximately 60% of the document correctly. The remaining 40% produced garbled output: clause numbers appeared as random digits, indented sub-clauses were merged into single run-on sentences, and the signature block text was entirely unrecognized. The resulting narration was incoherent for large sections — listening to the garbled output was not useful for understanding the contract terms.

**How We Fixed It:**

We re-scanned the document at 600 DPI with a flatbed scanner (rather than a phone camera), adjusted the contrast to pure black text on white background, and re-uploaded. The second OCR pass achieved roughly 95% accuracy. For critical documents like contracts, the lesson is clear: input quality directly determines output quality. Phone-camera scans of printed documents are not reliable enough for Speechify's OCR when comprehension matters.

## FAQ

### 1. Can Speechify read Chinese text?

Speechify supports Chinese text but with limited voice options compared with English. The available Chinese voices are functional but have noticeably less natural intonation than the English HD voices. For bilingual users switching between English and Chinese content, the quality gap is apparent — English narration sounds more human-like. This is an area where Speechify continues to invest, but at the time of our testing (August 2026), non-English voice quality remains a meaningful limitation.

### 2. Does Speechify work offline?

The Speechify mobile apps (iOS and Android) support offline listening after you pre-load content while connected. The web app and Chrome extension require an internet connection because voice generation happens server-side. To use Speechify offline, open the article or document in the mobile app while online, let it process, and it will be available for offline playback.

### 3. How does Speechify handle complex PDFs with tables and images?

Speechify reads the text layer of PDFs linearly — tables are read cell by cell in reading order, which can produce confusing output for complex multi-column layouts. Images are skipped unless they contain embedded text that OCR can extract. For PDFs with heavy formatting (multi-column layouts, dense tables, sidebars), the reading experience can be disjointed. Plain-text or single-column PDFs produce the most consistent results in our tests.

### 4. Is Speechify suitable for audiobook listening?

Speechify can read EPUB files and PDFs like audiobooks, and the HD voices are comfortable for extended listening. However, it is not a replacement for professionally narrated audiobooks because AI voices, while natural, lack the emotional range and character differentiation of human narrators. Speechify is better suited for non-fiction, textbooks, and articles where information delivery matters more than performance.

### 5. Can I use Speechify for commercial voiceover projects?

No. Speechify's voice library is licensed for personal listening and accessibility use, not for commercial voiceover production. The generated audio cannot be downloaded as MP3 files or used in videos, podcasts, or other published content. For commercial voiceover needs, tools like Murf or ElevenLabs offer voice synthesis with commercial licensing.
