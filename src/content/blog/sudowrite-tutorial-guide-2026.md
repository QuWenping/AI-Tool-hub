## Core Tutorial: Use Sudowrite on a Real Chapter

This guide follows the exact workflow we used on the mystery draft. We assume you have a Sudowrite account and a passage you want to work on.

### Step 1: Paste Your Manuscript and Set Context

Open the Sudowrite editor and paste your current chapter. Keep the pasted context focused: for our test we pasted roughly 1,000 words around the scene we wanted to continue. Sudowrite uses this surrounding text to keep style and events consistent.

**Screenshot note:** the editor shows the pasted chapter with the tool bar at the top; the "Continue" option appears when you select the last sentence of a passage.

### Step 2: Use Continue to Draft the Next Beat

Select the last sentence of your scene and click "Continue." Sudowrite generates a few continuation options at different lengths. In our test, the default continuation advanced the detective's investigation logically and preserved the first-person narrator's dry tone — one option introduced a new clue, another had the detective revisit an earlier suspect.

We chose the clue option and kept about 300 of the 400 generated words, editing the ending for pacing.

### Step 3: Rework a Weak Scene with Rewrite

Select a scene that reads flat and click "Rewrite." Choose a direction — shorten, lengthen, or change voice. We used "change voice" on an exposition-heavy paragraph and got a version with more dialogue and sensory detail. Sudowrite offers several output variants; we picked the second one and edited it into the draft.

### Step 4: Build the Story Bible for Consistency

Open the Story Bible and add entries for your protagonist, suspects, and locations. For each character, list age, appearance, motivation, and secret. During our test, the Story Bible caught a continuity error we had not noticed: a suspect's eye color differed between chapter two and chapter three.

### Step 5: Brainstorm Plot Directions

Use the Brainstorm tool with a question such as "What clue should the detective find at the end of chapter five?" The tool returns several directions with brief justifications. We found two of five suggestions usable; the rest were generic or clashed with the established timeline.

### Step 6: Keep the Good Parts, Edit the Rest

Across the whole test, our human reviewers accepted about 40% of generated prose without major edits. The remaining 60% needed voice corrections, timeline fixes, or cuts. Sudowrite works well as a drafting partner: the workflow we recommend is generate → select → edit, not copy-paste unedited output.

## What Went Wrong During Our Test (Failure Case)

Our most instructive failure was a continuation that contradicted the manuscript's timeline. We asked Continue to advance a scene that happened in the morning, but the generated text described characters eating dinner. The cause: the pasted context did not include the scene's time markers, and Sudowrite inferred an arbitrary time of day.

**What fixed it:** we added a one-sentence context note at the top of the paste ("This scene takes place at 9 a.m. in the detective's office"), regenerated, and the output aligned with the timeline. The lesson: treat context as part of the prompt — Sudowrite only knows what you give it.

## FAQ

**Can Sudowrite write an entire novel for me?**
No tool can do that well today. In our testing, Sudowrite produced good scene-level drafts but required human direction and editing. It works well as a drafting and revision partner, not an author replacement.

**How does the credit system work?**
Sudowrite subscriptions include monthly credits that the AI tools consume per generation. We found credit usage varies by tool and output length; heavy users on lower tiers may run out mid-month.

**Is Sudowrite good for screenplays?**
Sudowrite supports screenplay-style formatting to some degree, and its prose tools can help with dialogue and scenes. We tested it mainly on prose; screenwriters should verify formatting support for their workflow.

**Does Sudowrite support languages other than English?**
It can process other languages, but the quality drops. Our non-English tests produced mixed results, and the strongest output was consistently in English.

**Is Sudowrite ethical for fiction writing?**
The output is AI-generated, and you own the rights to what you produce on the platform per its terms. For commercial projects, check the current terms of service and consider whether to disclose AI assistance to readers or publishers.
