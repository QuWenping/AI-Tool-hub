## Core Tutorial: Creating Presentations with Gamma in 2026

### Step 1: Generating Your First Deck from a Prompt

Visit [gamma.app](https://gamma.app) and sign up. Gamma's free tier includes 400 AI credits — enough to generate approximately 4–6 full decks before you need to upgrade.

The simplest workflow starts with a text prompt:

1. Click "Create New" and select "Generate from prompt"
2. Type a one-line description: "Quarterly product update for an early-stage SaaS company: Q3 revenue grew 22%, launched mobile app, hired 3 engineers, Q4 priority is enterprise sales pipeline"
3. Select a theme from the visual picker (Gamma offers approximately 20 themes covering professional, creative, and minimal styles)
4. Click "Generate"

Gamma produces a structured deck with a title slide, section headers, content slides with auto-selected images, and a summary slide — all in under 30 seconds for a 10-slide deck.

**Screenshot description:** *Gamma interface showing the "Generate from prompt" dialog with a text prompt entered, theme thumbnails displayed in a grid below, and the "Generate" button highlighted.*

### Step 2: Importing a Document or URL for Document-to-Deck Conversion

Gamma's document-to-deck feature converts existing content into structured presentations:

1. Click "Create New" and select "Paste in text" or "Import from URL"
2. Paste a research document, meeting notes, or article URL
3. Gamma analyzes the content structure, identifies key sections, and builds a narrative flow

In our testing, importing a 2,000-word blog post produced a 12-slide deck with the main arguments correctly identified as section headers, supporting data pulled into bullet points, and images auto-selected based on content keywords. The result needed approximately 10 minutes of editing — significantly faster than building a deck from scratch.

**Screenshot description:** *Side-by-side view: a pasted document on the left with highlighted sections, and the generated deck preview on the right showing how each section became a slide with matching visuals.*

### Step 3: Editing in the Notion-Like Interface

Gamma's editor resembles Notion or Google Docs — type `/` to access a block menu with options for text, images, videos, charts, tables, and embedded web content. Key editing features:

- **Block-based editing**: Drag to reorder slides and content blocks within slides
- **Inline image swapping**: Click any auto-generated image to search for a replacement
- **Theme changing**: Switch themes mid-edit — Gamma reformats all slides while preserving content
- **Real-time collaboration**: Share a link and co-edit with team members, with comments and suggestions

The editor's strength is speed: manual layout adjustments that would take 5 minutes per slide in PowerPoint take 30 seconds in Gamma.

**Screenshot description:** *Gamma editor showing a slide with the `/` command menu open, displaying options for "Image," "Video," "Chart," "Table," "Embed," and "Layout." A collaborator's cursor is visible on the same slide with a comment thread on the right.*

### Step 4: Applying Custom Branding

On the Pro plan ($16/seat/month), you can upload brand assets that apply across all generated decks:

1. Navigate to "Brand Settings"
2. Upload your logo, select brand colors (primary and accent), and choose fonts (heading and body)
3. All subsequent generations automatically use your brand kit

In our test, after uploading a brand kit for a fictional SaaS company, 10 decks generated across different topics consistently used the correct colors, fonts, and logo placement — no manual adjustments needed per deck.

**Screenshot description:** *Brand Settings panel showing an uploaded logo, a color picker with two hex values selected, and font dropdown menus for heading and body text. Below, a preview of a slide with the brand styling applied.*

### Step 5: Sharing and Tracking with Analytics

Gamma offers two sharing modes:

- **Interactive web link**: Recipients view the deck in their browser with smooth scrolling and embedded media. On the Pro plan, engagement analytics show who viewed the deck, which slides they spent time on, and how far they scrolled.
- **Export**: Download as PPTX or PDF. PPTX exports preserve most formatting, though complex layouts with embedded media may require minor adjustments in PowerPoint.

In our sales pitch test, the analytics revealed that investors spent the most time on the traction slide and the team slide — insight that informed which slides to expand in future pitches.

**Screenshot description:** *Gamma analytics dashboard showing a bar chart of slide-by-slide view duration. The "Traction" slide shows the longest average view time (47 seconds), followed by "Team" (38 seconds), while "Appendix" shows the shortest (4 seconds).*

## Failure Case: The Missing Narrative Thread

**The Prompt:**

> "Generate a presentation explaining our company's pivot from B2C to B2B: why we made the decision, what data supported it, how we executed the transition, and the early results after 6 months."

**What Went Wrong:**

Gamma generated a visually polished 14-slide deck with strong individual slides. However, the narrative arc was disjointed — the "why" section jumped directly to "results" without explaining the execution phase. The auto-selected images, while relevant to individual slides, clashed stylistically: one slide used a flat illustration, the next used a photorealistic stock image, creating visual inconsistency across the deck.

**How We Fixed It:**

We used the outline editor (available in the left panel) to reorder slides — moving the "Execution" section between "Why" and "Results." We then bulk-replaced all auto-generated images by clicking each and searching for replacements with consistent keywords ("corporate professional team"). The fix took approximately 8 minutes and produced a coherent deck. This case demonstrates that Gamma's strength is speed to a first draft — not zero-edit perfection. Budget 10–15 minutes of post-generation editing for important decks.

## FAQ

### 1. Can Gamma replace PowerPoint for my team?

It depends on your use case. For fast internal decks (status updates, training modules, meeting presentations), Gamma is a strong replacement — it generates drafts in seconds and edits quickly. For external decks requiring pixel-perfect control, complex animations, or strict brand compliance templates, PowerPoint remains the standard. Many teams we spoke with use Gamma for first drafts and internal presentations, then move to PowerPoint only for high-stakes client or investor decks.

### 2. How does Gamma's document-to-deck feature work?

You paste text or import a URL, and Gamma's AI analyzes the content structure, identifies section breaks, key arguments, and supporting data, then maps them to slides with auto-selected images. In our testing, a 2,000-word research document produced a 12-slide deck with reasonable section identification. The feature works best with well-structured source material — documents with clear headings, bullet points, and logical flow produce the best results.

### 3. Can I export Gamma decks to PowerPoint?

Yes. Gamma exports to both PPTX and PDF formats. The PPTX export preserves most formatting including text, images, and basic layouts. Complex elements like embedded videos or interactive widgets may not transfer perfectly and may need minor adjustments in PowerPoint. For most standard business decks, the export fidelity is high enough that post-export editing is minimal.

### 4. Does Gamma support real-time collaboration?

Yes. Gamma supports real-time co-editing and threaded comments, similar to Google Docs or Notion. Multiple team members can work on the same deck simultaneously, leave comments on specific slides, and resolve feedback threads. This makes it suitable for distributed teams that need to iterate on presentations together.

### 5. What does the Pro analytics feature show?

Gamma Pro's engagement analytics track: (1) who viewed your shared deck link, (2) how long they spent on each slide, (3) how far they scrolled through the deck, and (4) whether they clicked any embedded links. This data is useful for sales teams tracking prospect engagement with pitch decks and for internal communicators verifying that stakeholders have read important updates.
